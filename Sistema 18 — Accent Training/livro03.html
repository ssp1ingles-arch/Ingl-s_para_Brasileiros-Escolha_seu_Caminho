Unit 1Conselhos gerais — o que você precisa aprender

Fatos básicos sobre o vocabulário do inglês, segundo o livro:

  • As palavras mais comuns do inglês são as palavras gramaticais — que você já conhece.
  • Há muitas palavras em inglês de que você não precisa.
  • Há palavras que você precisa apenas entender ao ler ou ouvir.
  • E há palavras que você precisa ser capaz de usar. É nesse último grupo que vale gastar a maior parte do tempo.

English vocabulary (o texto de amostra da unidade)

English vocabulary has a remarkable range, flexibility and adaptability. Thanks to the periods of contact with foreign languages and its readiness to coin new words out of old elements, English seems to have far more words in its core vocabulary than other languages. For example, alongside kingly (from Anglo-Saxon) we find royal (from French) and regal (from Latin). There are many such sets of words which add greatly to our opportunities to express subtle shades of meaning at various levels of style.

Repare na diferença de uso: você provavelmente só precisa entender o verbo to coin ("cunhar" uma palavra nova), mas precisa usar palavras como contact, add e opportunities.
Unit 1O que significa "saber" uma palavra

Unit 6Revisando — do vocabulário passivo para o ativo

A grande vantagem de revisar é fazer a palavra dar o salto do vocabulário receptivo (que você entende) para o ativo (que você usa). O livro sugere cinco formas de forçar essa passagem:

  • Escrever as palavras e expressões que você quer aprender numa frase sobre a sua própria vida e seus interesses no momento.
  • Fazer questão de usar as palavras novas na próxima aula, na lição de casa ou de outra forma.
  • Manter um learning diary anotando o que particularmente te interessa nas palavras aprendidas.
  • Ficar de olho nessas palavras durante a leitura geral em inglês — se encontrar alguma em uso, anote-a com o contexto.
  • Escrever um parágrafo ou uma história encadeando as palavras que você quer aprender.
Planeje: quantas palavras por semana (5? 10? 15? mais?), onde e quando estudá-las (no caminho do trabalho, antes do jantar, na cama) e com que frequência revisar (toda semana? a cada cinco unidades? uma vez por mês?).
Unit 7Adivinhar e explicar o significado

Inferir pelo contexto. Há várias pistas para deduzir o sentido de uma palavra desconhecida:

PistaComo funciona
seu conhecimento prévio da situaçãoSe você já sabe que houve um terremoto em Los Angeles, vai entender earthquake facilmente ao ouvir o noticiário.
o contexto imediato"Suzanna picked one tall yellow gladiolus to put in her new crystal vase." — mesmo sem nunca ter visto a palavra, fica óbvio que é um tipo de flor.
pistas gramaticais"Marsha is very superstitious." só pode ser adjetivo; "Jackie tiptoed gingerly down the stairs." só pode ser advérbio.
semelhança com palavras que você já sabeVocê pode nunca ter visto headscarf, mas o sentido sai fácil dos dois componentes.
estrutura — prefixo ou sufixoUm prefixo ou sufixo já entrega boa parte do sentido.
semelhança com a sua línguaSe sua primeira língua é de origem latina, muitas palavras do inglês vão se parecer com ela. Cuidado com os false friends — parecem significar o mesmo, mas têm sentido diferente.
Nem sempre vai dar para deduzir tudo — mas lembre que você não precisa entender cada palavra de um texto para entender o texto inteiro. Quando o sentido for crucial, use as pistas, arrisque um palpite e só depois confira no dicionário.

Unit 12Adjetivos compostos

Adjetivo composto tem duas partes, normalmente ligadas por hífen: well-dressed, never-ending, shocking-pink. O sentido em geral fica claro pelas palavras combinadas, e a segunda parte é muitas vezes um particípio presente ou passado.

Aparência física (a descrição exagerada do livro, da cabeça aos pés)

Tom was a curly-haired, sun-tanned, blue-eyed, rosy-cheeked, thin-lipped, broad-shouldered, left-handed, slim-hipped, long-legged, flat-footed young man, wearing an open-necked shirt, brand-new, tight-fitting jeans and open-toed sandals.

Caráter

Melissa was absent-minded [forgetful], easy-going [relaxed], good-tempered [cheerful], warm-hearted [kind] and quick-witted [intelligent] if perhaps a little big-headed [proud of herself], two-faced [hypocritical], self-centred [egotistical] and stuck-up [snobbish, colloquial] at times.

Um grupo especial tem preposição na segunda parte

  1. The workers' declaration of an all-out strike forced management to improve conditions.
  2. Once there were fields here but now it's a totally built-up area.
  3. That student's parents are very well-off but they don't give him much money and he is always complaining of being hard-up.
  4. I love these shoes and, although they're worn-out, I can't throw them away.
  5. This area was once prosperous but it now looks very run-down.

Unit 14Substantivos compostos (2) — verbo + preposição

Alguns compostos nascem de phrasal verbs: takeover ← to take over. Têm um tom mais informal e são especialíssimos no jornalismo. O plural se faz com s no fim: pin-ups.

No jornalismo

  1. In response to the pay offer, there was a walk-out at the factory. [strike]
  2. There is going to be a crack-down on public spending. [action to prevent]
  3. An inquiry into the recent break-out from the local prison has recommended measures to tighten security there. [escape]
  4. Last month saw a tremendous shake-up in personnel. [change]
  5. Last week we reported on the break-up of the mayor's marriage. [collapse]

Economia e negócios

  1. The takeover of one of our leading hotel chains has just been announced. [purchase by another company]
  2. We're trying to find some new outlets for our products. [places to sell]
  3. Take your things to the check-out to pay for them. [cash-desk]
  4. Cutbacks will be essential until the recession is over. [reductions]
  5. Our profit was £1000 on a turnover of £10,000. [money passing through a company]

Tecnologia e vida moderna

  1. What the computer produces depends on the input. [information that is put in]
  2. Output has increased thanks to new technology. [production]
  3. We have a rather rapid staff turnover. [change]
  4. Just after leaving school he went through the stage of being a dropout. [person who rejects society]
  5. The consequences of fallout from Chernobyl are still being felt. [radio-active dust in the atmosphere]
  6. I can easily get you a printout of the latest figures. [paper on which computer information has been printed]
  7. A breakthrough has been made in AIDS research. [important discovery]

Uso geral

  1. Many of the problems were caused by a breakdown in communications. [failure]
  2. The outlook for tomorrow is good — sunny in most places. [prospect]
  3. There are drawbacks as well as advantages to every situation. [negative aspects]
  4. The outcome of the situation was not very satisfactory. [conclusion]
  5. TV companies always welcome feedback from viewers. [comments]
  6. It was clear from the outset that the set-up would cause problems. [start; situation]
  7. We parked in a lay-by on the by-pass. [parking space at the side of a road; road avoiding the centre of a town]
  8. The outbreak of war took many people by surprise. [start of something unpleasant]
Muitos desses compostos têm um phrasal verb paralelo: the outbreak of war / war broke out. Ao encontrar um composto assim, procure o phrasal verb correspondente e aprenda os dois juntos.

Unit 21Tempo

Uma coisa antes da outra

  1. Before I went to work I fed the cat. [+ oração: o mais típico]
  2. Before going to work I wrote some letters. [+ forma -ing: estilo formal/escrito]
  3. I had written to her prior to meeting the committee. [formal/escrito]
  4. It was nice to be in Venice. Previously I'd only been to Rome. [bem formal; a versão informal seria "Before that, I…"]
  5. I was in the office from 2.30. I was out earlier on. [antes disso; bem informal]
  6. The city is now called Thatcherville. Formerly it was Grabtown. [bem formal, típico da escrita; usado quando algo mudou de nome ou estado]

Coisas acontecendo ao mesmo tempo

  1. While I waited, I read the newspaper. [ou, mais formal: While waiting…; esperar e ler acontecem juntos]
  2. As I was driving to work, I saw an accident. [As descreve o fundo quando algo acontece em primeiro plano. As não pode ser seguido imediatamente de -ing.]
  3. I saw her just as she was turning the corner. [o momento exato]
  4. She was entering at the very time / the very moment I was leaving. [mais fortes e precisos que as ou just as]
  5. Whenever I watch a sad film, I cry. [toda vez que]
During e throughout são diferentes: During the war, I lived in Dublin. × Throughout the war, food was rationed. — During não especifica quanto tempo dentro do período; throughout significa do começo ao fim do período.

Uma coisa depois da outra

  1. After I'd locked up, I went to bed. [ou, mais formal: After locking up; não se costuma dizer "After having locked up", que é estilo formalíssimo]
  2. First we went to the theatre. Then / After that, we had a meal.
  3. He fell ill and was admitted to hospital. He died soon afterwards. [after that e afterwards são intercambiáveis aqui]
  4. Following my visit to Beijing, I bought several books about China. [bem formal]

O momento em que

  1. When I'm rich and famous, I'll buy a yacht. [não diga "When I will be rich…"]
  2. As soon as we've packed we can leave. [imediatamente depois]
  3. Once we've finished we can go and have a coffee. [menos específico]
  4. The moment / the minute I saw his face I knew I'd met him before.
  5. I stayed in that hospital the time (that) I broke my leg.
  6. I met Polly at Ken's wedding. On that occasion she was with a different man. [mais formal]

Ligando dois períodos ou eventos

  1. Dinner will be ready in about an hour. In the meantime, relax and have a drink. [entre agora e a refeição]
  2. The new computers are arriving soon. Till then, we'll have to use the old ones.
  3. I last met him in 1985. Since then I haven't seen him.
  4. By the time I retire, I will have worked here 26 years. [não diga "By the time I will retire…"]

Unit 23Causa, razão, propósito e resultado

Causa e razão — além de because, since e as

  1. Owing to the icy conditions, the two lorries collided.
  2. Owing to the fact that the conditions were icy… [com oração completa, é obrigatório o "the fact that"]
  3. The collision was due to the icy conditions.
  4. The collision was caused by ice on the road.
  5. The cause of the collision was ice on the road.
As expressões abaixo são todas bem formais, mais adequadas à escrita.
ExpressãoExemploQuando se usa

Razões e propósitos — as frases abaixo são todas bem formais e mais frequentes na escrita

  1. Her reason for not going with us was that she had no money. [ou, menos formal: The reason (why) she didn't go with us was that…]
  2. I wonder what his motives were in sending that letter? [propósito]
  3. I wonder what prompted him to send that letter? [razão/causa]
  4. She wrote to the press with the aim of exposing the scandal. [propósito]
  5. I've invited you here with a view to resolving our differences. [soa um pouco mais indireto que "with the aim of"]
  6. He refused to answer on the grounds that his lawyer wasn't there. [razão]
  7. The purpose of her visit was to inspect the equipment.

Resultados

  1. He did no work. As a result / As a consequence / Consequently, he failed his exams. [as versões com consequence/consequently soam mais formais]
  2. The result/consequence of all these changes is that no-one is happy any more.
  3. His remarks resulted in everyone getting angry. [verbo + in]
  4. The events had an outcome that no-one could have predicted. [resultado de um processo, evento, reunião, discussão]
  5. The upshot of all these problems was that we had to start again. [menos formal que outcome]
  6. When the election results were announced, chaos ensued. [formalíssimo]
Ao anotar essas expressões, não esqueça a preposição que vai com cada uma: result in, consequence of.
Unit 24Concessão e contraste

Concessão é aceitar uma parte de uma ideia ou fato, mas pôr contra ela outro argumento mais importante: Although they were poor, they were independent. · He is a bit stupid. He's very kind, nevertheless.

Verbos de concessão

ExemploParáfrase e comentário
I acknowledge/accept that he has worked hard but it isn't enough.= concordo, mas… (accept é menos formal que acknowledge)
I admit I was wrong, but I still think we were right to doubt her.= aceito que sou culpado do que me acusam
I concede that you are right about the goal, but not the method.= você ganhou este ponto na discussão (bem formal, usado em debates)

Advérbios e locuções de contraste — as quatro primeiras são bem informais

  1. OK, you're sorry. That's all well and good, but how are you going to pay us back?
  2. You shouldn't seem so surprised. After all, I did warn you.
  3. It's all very well saying you love dogs, but who'll take it for walks if we do get one?
  4. He is boring, and he is rather cold and unfriendly, but, for all that, he is your uncle and we should invite him.

E estas são mais formais

  1. Admittedly, she put a lot of effort in, but it was all wasted.
  2. I expected Mr Widebody to be fat. The reverse was true.
  3. We're not almost there at all. Quite the opposite; we've got five miles to go yet.
  4. In Europe they use metric measurements. In contrast, the USA still uses many non-metric measurements.
  5. It's not actually raining now. On the other hand, it may rain later, so take the umbrella.
On the other hand significa "isso é verdade e isto também é, visto de outro ângulo". On the contrary é bem formal e significa "isso não é verdade, o oposto é": The medicine did little to help her. On the contrary, it made her feel even more sick.

Colocações prontas para marcar contraste

  1. When it comes to politics, Jim and Ann are poles apart.
  2. There's a world of difference between being a friend and a lover.
  3. There's a great divide between left and right wing in general.
  4. A yawning gap divides the rich and poor in many countries.
  5. There's a huge discrepancy between his ideals and his actions.

  1. They sell chairs, tables, beds, and so on / etc. [and so on é mais informal que etc.]
  2. It'll go to the committee, then to the board, then to another committee, and so on and so forth. [informal; sugere uma continuação longa]
  3. He was a good sportsman and an excellent musician into the bargain / to boot. [informal; enfatiza a combinação dos itens]

  3. In addition to his BA in History, he has a PhD in Sociology. [bem formal]

  6. Apart from her many other hobbies, she restores old racing cars.

Não diga "As well as she speaks French, she also speaks Japanese." Diga "As well as speaking French, she…" — essas expressões pedem substantivo ou -ing.

Unit 26Palavras que se referem ao texto

Unit 33Como contar o que é incontável

Muitos incontáveis viram singular e contável com a piece of ou a (little) bit of — e plural com pieces of / bits of.

Do crime à sentença — a narrativa completa, com as preposições certas

Bill committed a crime when he robbed a bank. Someone witnessed the crime and told the police. The police charged him with bank robbery. They also accused his twin brother, Ben, of being his accomplice.

The case came to court and they were tried. The trial did not last very long. Bill and Ben both pleaded not guilty in court. Their lawyer did her best to defend them but the prosecuting lawyer produced a very strong case against them.

After brief deliberations, the jury passed verdict on them. They decided that Bill was guilty but Ben was innocent. The judge acquitted Ben of any involvement in the robbery but sentenced Bill to three years in prison. He also had to pay a large fine. Bill served two years in prison [jail] but was released from prison a year early. He got time off for good behaviour.

São geralmente informais e trazem um comentário pessoal sobre a situação — muitas vezes humorístico ou irônico. Use com cuidado, nunca só para "soar fluente". Numa situação formal com quem você não conhece, não diga "Do take the weight off your feet" — diga "Do sit down" ou "Have a seat".
