Estrutura 01

Sujeito simples e sujeito completo

O simple subject é a palavra principal; o complete subject é ela mais tudo o que a descreve. O teste: se você tirar a palavra e a frase perder o sentido, achou o sujeito simples.

FraseSujeito completoSujeito simples
The seats on the bus are very comfortable.The seats on the busseats
The first reporters on the scene did not get all the facts.The first reporters on the scenereporters
The late Amelia Earhart was a pioneer in aviation.The late Amelia EarhartAmelia Earhart
The famous Mona Lisa is a painting by Leonardo da Vinci.The famous Mona LisaMona Lisa
Did a letter from your sister come this morning?a letter from your sisterletter
Farther up on the hill is a house with white shutters.a house with white shuttershouse
Asleep in the crib was a six-month-old baby.a six-month-old babybaby
My older brother is graduating in June.My older brotherbrother

Estrutura 04

Verbo de ação — físico e mental — e verbo de ligação

Há dois tipos de action verb: o que expressa ação física (dá para ver ou ouvir) e o que expressa ação mental (acontece na cabeça). O que não é ação é linking verb.

ação física
The car skidded, left the road, and smashed into a telephone pole.
O carro derrapou, saiu da pista e bateu num poste.
Pat tagged the runner.
O Pat encostou no corredor.
Open a window.
Abra uma janela.
Who rang the bell?
Quem tocou a campainha?
ação mental
We believed and trusted them because we knew them.
A gente acreditava e confiava neles porque a gente os conhecia.
Did you understand the lesson?
Você entendeu a aula?
I forgot the combination.
Eu esqueci a combinação.
He considers me his best friend.
Ele me considera o melhor amigo dele.
We are hoping for the best this season.
A gente está esperando o melhor nesta temporada.
verbo de ligação
Jordan is angry at us.
O Jordan está bravo com a gente.
Estrutura 05

Verbos auxiliares e a locução verbal

Um verbo principal pode carregar até três auxiliares. Em perguntas, o sujeito vai depois do primeiro auxiliar.

Quantos auxiliaresExemplo

doisThe letters have been mailed.
trêsThey should have been mailed earlier.
AfirmaçãoPergunta

O auxiliar sozinho evita a repetição — é o que deixa a frase enxuta:
RepetitivoEnxuto

Nas contrações de verbo + not, o o de not desaparece. E ain't, apesar de comum na fala, não é forma padrão.

Estrutura 07

Substantivo comum e substantivo próprio

O proper noun nomeia algo único e leva sempre maiúscula. O common noun não leva. E um substantivo pode ter mais de uma palavra: living room, post office.

FraseComunsPróprios
Bourbon Street is a lively street in New Orleans.streetBourbon Street · New Orleans
The Brooklyn Bridge is the oldest bridge over the East River.bridgeBrooklyn Bridge · East River
An ocean is a vast body of water.ocean · body · water—
The Pacific Ocean is a vast body of water.body · waterPacific Ocean
substantivo de mais de uma palavra
The driver stopped at the post office to mail a package.
O motorista parou no correio para postar um pacote.
Divers fish for pearls in the Gulf of Mexico.
Mergulhadores pescam pérolas no Golfo do México.
Estrutura 08

Plural dos substantivos — as oito regras

Oito regras dão conta de quase todos os plurais. O resto é lista de irregulares para decorar.

RegraExemplos
1. Acrescente shand → hands
2. Termina em s, sh, ch, x, z → eswish → wishes · class → classes · dish → dishes
3. Termina em -f → troque por -vesleaf → leaves · loaf → loaves · half → halves · self → selves · shelf → shelves · thief → thieves · wolf → wolves
4. Termina em -fe → troque o f por v e some sknife → knives · life → lives · wife → wives
5. Vogal + y → só sjourney → journeys · turkey → turkeys
6. Consoante + y → ieslady → ladies · enemy → enemies · spy → spies · factory → factories · penny → pennies
7. Vogal + o → spatio → patios
8. Consoante + o → s na maioria dos casospiano → pianos
IrregularesSingular → Plural
mudança de vogalman → men · woman → women · foot → feet · tooth → teeth · louse → lice · mouse → mice · goose → geese
terminação -enchild → children · ox → oxen · grandchild → grandchildren
Estrutura 09

Substantivo possessivo — e a economia que ele traz

Duas regras: possuidor singular → apóstrofo + s; possuidor plural terminado em s → só apóstrofo. E o genitivo encurta muito a frase.

Em vez de escreverEscreva
the car that my brother has (6 palavras)my brother's car (3 palavras)
PossuidorRegraExemplo
singular'sthe boy's bike · the class's project
plural terminado em ssó o apóstrofothe boys' bikes · the teachers' lounge
plural sem s'sthe children's toys · the men's room
Estrutura 10

Sujeito composto e predicado composto

Dois ou mais sujeitos com o mesmo verbo formam um compound subject. Dois ou mais verbos com o mesmo sujeito formam um compound predicate.

sujeito composto com and
Erin and Sean joined the team.
A Erin e o Sean entraram para o time.
sujeito composto com or
Nickels, dimes, or quarters may be deposited.
Moedas de cinco, dez ou vinte e cinco centavos podem ser depositadas.
Sweaters and jackets are on sale.
Suéteres e jaquetas estão em promoção.
Leah and the twins supported the new cafeteria regulations.
A Leah e os gêmeos apoiaram as novas regras da cantina.
Duas frasesUma só, com predicado composto
I visited the local library. I found the latest issue.I visited the local library and found the latest issue.
Gone with the Wind was rejected many times. It became a bestseller.Gone with the Wind was rejected many times but became a bestseller.
Estrutura 11

Objeto direto e objeto indireto

O direct object recebe a ação do verbo. O indirect object é a quem ou para quem a ação é feita — e vem antes do objeto direto.

objeto direto
Pat tagged the runner.
O Pat encostou no corredor.
Who rang the bell?
Quem tocou a campainha?
I forgot the combination.
Eu esqueci a combinação.
objeto indireto
I gave my sister the book.
Eu dei o livro para minha irmã.
She sent us a postcard.
Ela mandou um cartão-postal para a gente.
He told me the truth.
Ele me contou a verdade.
Estrutura 12

Pronomes e antecedentes

O antecedent é o substantivo que o pronome substitui. Se o antecedente não estiver claro, a frase confunde.

ConfusoClaro
Linda told her mother that she had left the lawn sprinklers on.Linda told her mother, "I left the lawn sprinklers on."
Linda told her mother, "You left the lawn sprinklers on."
Use that towel to wipe your face and put it in the clothes hamper.Use that towel to wipe your face and put the towel in the clothes hamper.
If papers are left behind by sloppy hikers, burn them.Any papers left behind by sloppy hikers should be burned.

Estrutura 14

Concisão — cortar o que não acrescenta

Boa escrita é escrita enxuta. Corte a redundância, o pleonasmo e o rebuscamento.

ProlixoConciso
At the soccer match, Joel met up with two friends from elementary school.At the soccer match, Joel met two friends from elementary school.
We flew by air to Cleveland and returned back by bus.We flew to Cleveland and returned by bus.
The newscast which was televised this morning on a local station described a fire.This morning's local television newscast described a fire.
The young, immature baby of but a year walked unsteadily across the floor.The one-year-old tottered across the floor into her mother's arms.
The feline member of our family loves to frolic and gambol with the canine. (pretensioso)Our cat often plays with the family dog.
Estrutura 15

Clareza — modificador solto e palavra imprecisa

Um modificador tem de ficar perto do que ele modifica, e o alvo tem de estar na frase. E a palavra específica sempre vale mais que a genérica.

ConfusoClaro
Turning the corner, the post office was on the left.Turning the corner, I noticed the post office on my left.
As I turned the corner, I noticed the post office on my left.
Tracy saw a deer riding her bike through the Hopkins meadow.Riding her bike through the Hopkins meadow, Tracy saw a deer.
At the age of three, Mario's mother remarried.When Mario was three, his mother remarried.
While working in the library, a new shipment of books arrived.While I was working in the library, a new shipment of books arrived.
I wanted to go skating, and Angela preferred hiking.I wanted to go skating, but Angela preferred hiking.
Because Ben was on a visit to his ill grandmother, he was cheerful.Although Ben was on a visit to his ill grandmother, he was cheerful.
Our Saturday project is to install all broken windows.Our Saturday project is to replace all broken windows.
Don't fail to miss tonight's rerun of The Simpsons.Don't miss tonight's rerun. · Don't fail to see tonight's rerun.
VagoEspecífico
We walked up a steep peak and rejoiced at the top.We clambered up the cone of Mt. Katahdin and shouted for joy.
On our western trip, we visited three national parks.On our western trip, we visited Rocky Mountain, Yellowstone, and Yosemite.
We had several different kinds of transportation.We tried horseback riding, canoeing, and helicoptering.
Estrutura 16

Variar a frase — comprimento, tipo e abertura

Frase boa é frase variada. Varie o comprimento, o tipo (afirmação, pergunta, exclamação, ordem, pedido) e, sobretudo, a palavra com que a frase começa.

ComprimentoExemplo
curtaThrow caution to the wind.
médiaIf we had wings, we would soar like eagles.
longaOnly one thing is better than winning, and that is winning well.
Tipo de fraseExemplo
PerguntaWhere did I leave my tennis racket?
ExclamaçãoI just remembered — the public courts!
OrdemCall the pro shop and say I'm on my way.
Pedido educadoPlease let my mother know I'll be late.
Começando por…Exemplo
advérbioWearily, the home team went onto the field.
thereThere are still many undiscovered galaxies.
frase preposicionalContrary to popular belief, a person's hair cannot turn white overnight.
particípioFinding the passage blocked, the cave explorers turned back.
infinitivoTo melt its way through spring snows, the skunk cabbage produces its own heat.
apostoA language expert for the United Nations, Georges spoke six languages.
Sem apostoCom aposto
The eagle, which is the U.S. national symbol, won out over the turkey.The eagle, the U.S. national symbol, won out over the turkey.
The medieval dinner plate was a thick slice of bread called a trencher.The medieval dinner plate, the trencher, was a thick slice of bread.
Estrutura 17

Fragmento de frase — o que falta para virar frase

Um fragment é um pedaço que não fecha: falta sujeito, falta verbo, ou é só uma oração subordinada solta.

O que éFragmentoFrase completa
falta verboRaquel, along with a friend of hers from Miami.Raquel arrived, along with a friend of hers from Miami.
falta verboA small puppy running across the lawn.A small puppy was running across the lawn.
falta sujeitoTook the train from Boston to New Haven.He took the train from Boston to New Haven.
frase preposicional soltaNear the monitor on the desk.An owner's manual lay near the monitor on the desk.
oração subordinada soltaUnless you are here by six o'clock.Unless you are here by six o'clock, I will leave without you.
oração subordinada soltaWhen suddenly the traffic on Main Street stopped.My family was driving home when suddenly the traffic on Main Street stopped.
oração subordinada soltaWhen Julia won.Julia won. · I was happy when Julia won.
particípio sem verboDenyce jumping up and down with her diploma.Denyce was jumping up and down with her diploma. · Denyce jumped up and down with her diploma.
particípio sem verboJaleel packing his gear for the rafting adventure.Jaleel packed his gear. · Packing his gear for the rafting adventure, Jaleel whistled.
gerúndio soltoWinning the soccer match with a penalty kick.Argentina won the soccer match with a penalty kick.
infinitivo soltoTo pick blackberries for a pie.Cara decided to pick blackberries for a pie. · Cara picked blackberries for a pie.
Estrutura 18

Frase emendada (run-on) — e as quatro saídas

Duas ideias completas grudadas sem nada, ou grudadas só por vírgula, formam um run-on. Há quatro consertos: ponto final, conjunção coordenativa, conjunção subordinativa ou ponto e vírgula.

Run-onConserto
I enjoy computer games they keep me entertained.I enjoy computer games. They keep me entertained. (duas frases)
There was a loud splash, the dog had jumped in.There was a loud splash. The dog had jumped in. (duas frases)
Video games require good coordination, playing them improves it.Video games require good coordination, and playing them improves it. (composta)
Britney goes to summer school, she has to make up a course.Britney goes to summer school because she has to make up a course. (complexa)
The driver stopped, then he got out of his car.The driver stopped. Then he got out of his car.
My best subject is science, therefore I took a lab course.My best subject is science; therefore, I took a lab course. (ponto e vírgula)
Estrutura 19

Usar o tempo certo — e não misturar tempos

Os seis tempos, cada um com sua função. E a regra que resolve a maioria dos erros: dentro da mesma frase, mantenha o mesmo tempo.

TempoExemplo
PresentA statue of Thomas Jefferson stands in the memorial.
PastThe Tampa Bay Buccaneers won the Super Bowl.
FutureTaryn will go to the dentist at five o'clock.
Present perfectThe Pyramids have lasted for thousands of years.
Past perfectBy late afternoon, all the guests had left.
Future perfectBy the year 2020, people's taste in popular music will have changed.
Tempos misturadosTempo consistente
I lose my keys and waited for my mother to come home.I lose my keys and wait for my mother to come home.
I lost my keys and waited for my mother to come home.
Because she misses the bus, she walked to school.Because she misses the bus, she walks to school.
Because she missed the bus, she walked to school.

Estrutura 21

Concordância entre sujeito e verbo — as cinco armadilhas

O verbo concorda com o sujeito em número. As dificuldades aparecem quando há palavras entre os dois, quando o sujeito é composto, ou quando o verbo vem antes do sujeito.

PessoaSingularPlural
1ªI enjoy · I am · I was · I have · I hadwe enjoy · we are · we were · we have · we had
2ªyou enjoy · you are · you were · you have · you hadyou enjoy · you are · you were · you have · you had
3ªhe, she, it enjoys · is · was · has · hadthey enjoy · are · were · have · had
ArmadilhaExemploPor quê
1. Palavras entre sujeito e verboA tree (with green leaves) stands at the front gate.o sujeito é tree, não leaves
2. with, together with, as well as, including, plus, no less than não contamThe coach, as well as the players, is going to the game by plane.o sujeito é coach
3a. Dois sujeitos ligados por and → pluralHigh seas and dense fog have slowed the rescue operation. 
3b. Dois singulares ligados por or / nor → singularAn apple or an orange was in every box lunch. 
3c. either… or / neither… nor → concorda com o mais próximoNeither Stacy nor her brothers are going.
Either these telephone numbers or that address is wrong.		 
4. You sempre leva verbo pluralYou were listed on today's honor roll. · Were you expecting the honor?You was não existe
5. Verbo antes do sujeito → ache o sujeito primeiroWere the nominees for Best Actor all present?o sujeito é nominees
